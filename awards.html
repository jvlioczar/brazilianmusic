Brazilian awards, festivals and talent shows

How Brazil recognizes its music: awards, the festivals that put it on stage, the talent shows that revealed artists and the TV programmes that brought music into people's homes.

AwardsFestivals and stagesTV talent showsMusic TV programmes

Awards

Since 2000

Grammy Latino

Organizer: Latin Recording Academy (US)

It has categories dedicated to Portuguese-language music: best rock or alternative album, contemporary pop, MPB, samba/pagode, sertanejo, roots, Portuguese-language song, urban performance and Christian music. It is the most prestigious international award for Brazilian artists.

Since 1994

Prêmio Multishow de Música Brasileira

Organizer: Multishow channel (Globo)

The country's most visible popular award, with public and expert-jury votes; televised ceremony every year.

Since 1987

Prêmio da Música Brasileira

Organizer: Independent production (José Maurício Machline)

Formerly Prêmio Sharp and Prêmio TIM; the most traditional, with a critics' jury and categories by genre. Each edition honours a great name.

Since 1995

MTV Video Music Brasil (VMB)

Organizer: MTV Brasil (1995–2012)

For almost two decades, the award that defined Brazilian pop and rock for the young.

Since 1958

Troféu Imprensa

Organizer: SBT (Silvio Santos)

A TV award with best male and female singer categories, voted by journalists and the public.

Since 2017

WME Awards

Organizer: Women's Music Event

An award dedicated to women in Brazilian music, across every role in the industry.

Since 2015

Prêmio Profissionais da Música

Organizer: Independent

Recognizes producers, engineers, backing musicians and other backstage professionals.

Since 2002

Prêmio Jovem Brasileiro

Organizer: Independent

Aimed at a young audience; music, TV and internet categories.

Since 2011

Troféu Promessas

Organizer: Geo Eventos and TV Globo

A gospel music award held with TV Globo's support from 2011 to 2013.

Since 1959

Grammy

Organizer: Recording Academy (US)

The American award where “Getz/Gilberto” was Album of the Year in 1965; Sérgio Mendes, Caetano Veloso and Gilberto Gil have also won.

Festivals and stages

Since 1907 · Recife / Olinda

Carnaval de Recife e Olinda

Frevo, maracatu and the Galo da Madrugada, the planet's biggest street bloco.

Since 1932 · Rio de Janeiro

Carnaval do Rio

Samba school parades at the Sambadrome (since 1984) and hundreds of street blocos.

Since 1950 · Salvador

Carnaval de Salvador

Trios elétricos on the Barra-Ondina and Campo Grande circuits; the world's biggest street party according to Guinness.

Since 1955 · Barretos (SP)

Festa do Peão de Barretos

Latin America's biggest rodeo and sertanejo's main stage.

Since 1965 · Parintins (AM)

Festival de Parintins

The Garantido and Caprichoso bulls face off at the Bumbódromo, in the middle of the Amazon.

Since 1965 · São Paulo

Festival da TV Excelsior

The 1965 festival where Elis Regina sang “Arrastão” and became the great revelation of Brazilian song.

Since 1966 · Rio de Janeiro

Festival Internacional da Canção (FIC)

Held by Globo until 1972; it launched 'Sabiá', 'Pra Não Dizer Que Não Falei das Flores' and 'BR-3'.

Since 1966 · São Paulo

Festival de MPB da Record

Until 1969; 'A Banda', 'Disparada', 'Alegria, Alegria' and 'Ponteio' came out of here.

Since 1970 · Campos do Jordão (SP)

Festival de Inverno de Campos do Jordão

The country's great annual classical music festival, every July.

Since 1983 · Campina Grande (PB) / Caruaru (PE)

São João de Campina Grande e Caruaru

Thirty days of forró; the two cities compete for the title of 'biggest São João in the world'.

Since 1985 · Rio de Janeiro

Rock in Rio

Created by Roberto Medina; the 1985 edition launched BRock and the festival became a global franchise (Lisbon, Madrid, Las Vegas).

Since 1985 · Rio de Janeiro / São Paulo

Free Jazz Festival

Sixteen editions between 1985 and 2001, with jazz and Brazilian music side by side.

Since 1988 · Rio de Janeiro / São Paulo

Hollywood Rock

Conceived by Nelson Motta; after a one-off edition in 1975, it ran from 1988 to 1996.

Since 1993 · Recife

Abril Pro Rock

The festival that revealed manguebeat.

Since 1995 · Recife

Rec-Beat

An independent-music stage inside Recife carnival.

Since 1996 · Xangri-lá (RS)

Planeta Atlântida

Summer festival on the Rio Grande do Sul coast.

Since 1999 · Salvador

Festival de Verão Salvador

Showcase of axé and Bahian pop.

Since 1999 · Goiânia

Festival Bananada

The festival that made Goiânia a capital of indie and stoner.

Since 2000 · Pratigi (BA)

Universo Paralello

A beach psytrance festival at New Year, with an international crowd.

Since 2000 · São Paulo

Skol Beats

The country's biggest electronic music festival of its time, from 2000 to 2008.

Since 2002 · Ribeirão Preto (SP)

João Rock

One of the biggest Brazilian rock festivals outside the capitals.

Since 2003 · Rio de Janeiro / São Paulo

TIM Festival

The successor to Free Jazz, with jazz, indie, electronic music and rock.

Since 2007 · São Paulo

Planeta Terra Festival

An indie and alternative festival held from 2007 to 2013.

Since 2011 · Goiânia

Villa Mix

A sertanejo universitário mega-festival.

Since 2012 · São Paulo

Lollapalooza Brasil

The Brazilian edition of the American festival, at the Interlagos racetrack.

Since 2014 · São Paulo

Coala Festival

Dedicated solely to Brazilian music, with unprecedented cross-generational meetings.

Since 2023 · São Paulo

The Town

Rock in Rio's sister festival, at Interlagos.

TV talent shows 13

Globo, SBT · 2012–2023, 2025–

The Voice Brasil

Twelve seasons on Globo, from 2012 to 2023; coaches over the years included Carlinhos Brown, Claudia Leitte, Lulu Santos, Daniel, Michel Teló, Ivete Sangalo, Iza and Gaby Amarantos, and Michel Teló won as a coach seven times. In 2025 the show moved to SBT, where Thiago Garcia, from Mumuzinho's team, won.

Launched: Ellen Oléria, Sam Alves, Danilo Reis & Rafael, Renato Vianna, Mylena Jardim, Samantha Ayara, Léo Pain, Tony Gordon, Victor Alves, Giuliano Eriston, Keilla Júnia, Ivan Barreto, Thiago Garcia, Ju Moraes, Liah Soares, Lucy Alves, Romero Ribeiro, Ayrton Montarroyos, Nikki Valentine, Carol Biazin, Day, Vinícius D'Black, Hugo Rafael
Globo · 2016–2023

The Voice Kids

The children's version, with coaches such as Carlinhos Brown, Ivete Sangalo, Simone & Simaria, Michel Teló, Gaby Amarantos and Mumuzinho.

Globo · 2021–2022

The Voice +

The version for singers over 60.

SBT · 2002–2003

Popstars

Formed Rouge (2002), Brazil's biggest girl group, and the boy band Br'oz (2003).

Launched: Rouge, Br'oz

Fama

A music reality show in the Spanish 'Operación Triunfo' format.

Ídolos

The Brazilian 'Pop Idol'; judges included Rick Bonadio, Arnaldo Saccomani, Marco Camargo and Paula Lima. Thaeme (2007) went on to sertanejo with Thaeme & Thiago.

Launched: Thaeme
SBT · 2008–2013

Astros

A newcomers' talent show hosted by Marcos Mion and Rodrigo Faro.

Globo · 2014–2016

SuperStar

A band competition with app voting; winners were Malta (2014), Dois Africanos (2015) and Versalle (2016). Scalene was runner-up in 2015.

Launched: Malta, Scalene, Versalle

Launched: Ravena

The 'All Together Now' format with 100 judges, hosted by Rodrigo Faro; it has a Teen version.

The Masked Singer Brasil
