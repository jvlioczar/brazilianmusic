Bossa Nova

Since 1958Where Rio de JaneiroSubgenres 1Artists 24

Slowed-down samba, jazz harmony and a near-whispered voice: bossa nova was born in Rio's Zona Sul apartments with João Gilberto, Tom Jobim and Vinicius de Moraes, and in 1964 'The Girl from Ipanema' made it the most-heard Brazilian music on the planet.

If you like…

If you like vocal jazz, cool jazz or lounge, here is the origin of much of that sound.

  1. 1958

    Canção do Amor Demais

    Songs by Tom and Vinicius with João Gilberto's guitar on two tracks; an early bossa nova landmark.

  2. 1959

    Chega de Saudade

    João Gilberto

    The record that opens bossa nova: syncopated guitar and whispered voice.

  3. 1960

    O Amor, o Sorriso e a Flor

    João Gilberto

    Second LP, with "Samba de Uma Nota Só" and "Corcovado".

  4. 1961

    João Gilberto

    João Gilberto

    Third LP, closing bossa nova's founding trilogy.

  5. 1963

    The Composer of Desafinado, Plays

    Tom Jobim

    Jobim's first instrumental record in the US, arranged by Claus Ogerman.

  6. 1964

    Getz/Gilberto

    Stan Getz & João Gilberto

    Took bossa nova to the world, with "The Girl from Ipanema" and Astrud Gilberto.

  7. 1964

    Nara

    Nara Leão

    Debut, between bossa nova and hillside samba.

  8. 1966

    Herb Alpert Presents Sergio Mendes & Brasil '66

    Sérgio Mendes

    The version of "Mas Que Nada" heard around the world.

  9. 1966

    Os Afro-Sambas

    Baden Powell e Vinicius de Moraes

    One of the 100 greatest Brazilian albums (Rolling Stone Brasil, 2007).

    Frank Sinatra & Tom Jobim

    Sinatra sings Jobim, with Jobim himself on guitar.

  11. 1967

    Wave

  12. 1973

    João Gilberto

    João Gilberto

    The "white album": voice, guitar and minimal percussion.

  13. 1973

    Matita Perê

    Tom Jobim

    One of the 100 greatest Brazilian albums (Rolling Stone Brasil, 2007).

  14. 1974

    Elis Regina & Tom Jobim

  15. 1977
